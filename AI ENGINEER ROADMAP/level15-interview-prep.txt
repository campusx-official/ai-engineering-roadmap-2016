Level 15 · GenAI / AI Engineer Interview Prep

Scope: convert everything learned across the roadmap into interview performance. This level does not re-teach concepts; it focuses on scenario-based questions, system-design discussions, debugging cases, trade-offs, and defending real engineering decisions under interview conditions.

M15.1 · Interview strategy & role positioning — (sessions: 1)

Understand how GenAI interviews have shifted from theory-heavy questioning toward system design, debugging, architecture, and production scenarios.
Learn to read a job description and identify which roadmap areas are actually being tested instead of trying to prepare every listed tool.
Distinguish AI Engineer, GenAI Data Scientist, and traditional Data Scientist roles by emphasis rather than treating them as completely separate disciplines.
Understand why modern AI-engineering interviews increasingly emphasize the engineering layer: APIs, inference, LLMOps, observability, networking, cloud, reliability, and deployment.
Practice explaining API-hosted models vs self-hosted open-weight models across capability, cost, latency, control, privacy, and operational burden.
Build a personalized interview-preparation plan based on target role and current weaknesses.
Outcome: Prioritize interview preparation intelligently and position your existing skillset for the role being targeted.

M15.2 · Production RAG interview scenarios — (sessions: 2)

Design ingestion pipelines for messy enterprise data containing complex PDFs, nested tables, diagrams, varying layouts, and continuously changing documents.
Whiteboard a RAG system serving large numbers of concurrent users across very large document collections.
Separate the architecture into services such as embedding, retrieval, LLM inference, ingestion, orchestration, and storage.
Explain why application/orchestration services should often remain stateless while persistent state belongs in databases, object stores, vector databases, graph databases, caches, or search indexes.
Reason about production infrastructure around RAG:
asynchronous messaging and queues,
service fallbacks,
object/vector/graph/search storage,
observability and SLAs,
networking and IAM,
secrets and encryption,
CI/CD and deployment.
Practice debugging scenarios such as: “The RAG system worked well for a week and then quality dropped. How would you investigate?”
Isolate failures across ingestion, retrieval, generation, data changes, model changes, and infrastructure.
Outcome: Whiteboard and defend a production-scale RAG architecture rather than describing a demo-level retriever → LLM pipeline.

M15.3 · Agentic AI interview scenarios — (sessions: 2)

Design an agentic system for a concrete business task involving planning, tools, memory, and multi-step execution.
Defend the choice between a deterministic workflow and an autonomous agent.
Reason about tools, MCP integrations, memory, and multi-agent architectures under realistic constraints.
Handle common interview failure scenarios:
runaway loops,
poor tool selection,
tool failures,
nondeterministic trajectories,
excessive token/cost usage,
context degradation,
overly powerful tool permissions.
Design human-in-the-loop checkpoints and guardrails for consequential actions.
Debug an agent from its trajectory when it succeeds in testing but fails under production conditions.
Explain when not to introduce multi-agent or autonomous behavior.
Outcome: Design and defend an agent architecture while reasoning explicitly about autonomy, cost, reliability, recovery, and blast radius.

M15.4 · Fine-tuning & model adaptation interviews — (sessions: 1)

Make the prompt vs RAG vs fine-tuning decision from a concrete product brief.
Explain when fine-tuning creates meaningful value and when it adds unnecessary training and deployment complexity.
Discuss LoRA, QLoRA, PEFT, and quantization in terms of engineering trade-offs rather than only definitions.
Reason about deploying a fine-tuned open-weight model: serving architecture, GPU requirements, inference optimization, latency, and cost.
Practice the debugging case: “The fine-tuned model performed well before deployment, but production accuracy dropped. What do you investigate?”
Consider training data, formatting, distribution shift, evaluation regressions, inference configuration, serving changes, and quantization artifacts.
Outcome: Defend a model-adaptation strategy and systematically debug performance degradation after deployment.

M15.5 · LLMOps & production debugging interviews — (sessions: 2)

Diagnose production incidents involving model APIs, self-hosted inference, retrieval services, queues, caches, rate limits, and downstream tools.
Reason through latency spikes, provider outages, cost explosions, capacity limits, and degraded model quality.
Discuss fallback strategies, graceful degradation, retries, load balancing, and autoscaling.
Interpret monitoring signals such as latency, throughput, error rates, token usage, cost, retrieval quality, and eval scores.
Explain what should trigger an alert and what requires rollback versus investigation.
Practice comparing proprietary API deployment with self-hosted inference under realistic traffic and privacy constraints.
Outcome: Demonstrate that you can operate and troubleshoot an AI system after it leaves the notebook.

M15.6 · AI system-design interviews — (sessions: 2)

Practice turning an ambiguous product requirement into explicit assumptions around users, traffic, latency, quality, cost, freshness, privacy, and reliability.
Start with the simplest viable architecture before introducing RAG, agents, fine-tuning, routing, caching, or complex infrastructure.
Whiteboard systems using the full AI stack:
model selection/routing,
RAG and context,
tools/agents,
caches,
databases,
inference services,
observability,
evals,
security,
deployment.
Design under enterprise constraints where a public API is not allowed, and defend a managed platform or self-hosted deployment on identity, residency, capacity, and cost.
Explain every major trade-off rather than merely naming technologies.
Practice system designs such as:
enterprise support assistant,
coding assistant,
research agent,
large-scale document intelligence system.
Outcome: Run a structured AI-system-design interview from requirements through architecture, bottlenecks, failure modes, and scaling.

M15.7 · Staying current without chasing hype — (sessions: 1)

Build a defensible process for evaluating new models, frameworks, papers, benchmarks, and tooling.
Separate meaningful capability/infrastructure changes from short-lived hype.
Explain how you would evaluate a newly released model before replacing an existing production model.
Use benchmarks → shortlist → custom evals → cost/latency comparison → controlled rollout rather than social-media consensus.
Develop a strong answer to common interview questions such as “How do you stay current in GenAI?”
Outcome: Demonstrate informed judgment rather than simply listing the latest tools.

M15.8 · Project & portfolio defense — (sessions: 2)

Prepare 2–3 major projects from the roadmap for deep technical questioning.
Explain each project using:
problem → requirements → baseline → architecture → decisions → trade-offs → failures → evals → production results.
Be prepared to explain why you selected a particular model, retriever, agent architecture, framework, database, or deployment strategy.
Quantify where possible: quality scores, latency, cost/query, throughput, dataset size, traffic, or improvement over baseline.
Discuss failures and design mistakes openly and explain what you changed as a result.
Prepare for interviewer follow-ups such as:
“Why didn't you use RAG?”
“Why not fine-tune?”
“Why do you need an agent?”
“What happens at 100× traffic?”
“How would you reduce the cost by 50%?”
“What breaks first?”
Outcome: Defend projects as engineering decisions rather than merely demonstrate that they work.

M15.9 · Behavioral communication for AI engineers — (sessions: 1)

Communicate technical reasoning clearly without drowning the interviewer in implementation details.
State assumptions before solving ambiguous design questions.
Think in structured trade-offs rather than jumping directly to a tool or architecture.
Use diagrams and whiteboards to progressively reveal a design.
When unsure, explain how you would measure, experiment, or debug rather than bluffing.
Structure experience-based answers around the situation, decision, implementation, result, and lessons learned.
Outcome: Make strong technical thinking visible to the interviewer.
M15.10 · Capstone: Full Mock GenAI Interview Loop — (sessions: 2)

Simulate a complete AI-engineering interview.

Round 1 · Scenario & debugging

Solve rapid production scenarios such as:

RAG accuracy suddenly drops.
Agent enters an expensive loop.
Model provider latency doubles.
Fine-tuned model regresses after deployment.
Prompt change improves quality but doubles cost.
Retriever works offline but fails on real production queries.
Round 2 · System design

Receive an ambiguous product brief and:

Clarify requirements → define constraints → establish baseline → design architecture → choose models → design data/context layer → handle scale → add reliability/security → define evals/monitoring → defend trade-offs.

Round 3 · Project defense

Present one major portfolio project and handle increasingly deep follow-up questions on architecture, alternatives, costs, failures, evals, and scaling.

Final result: demonstrate that you can reason like an AI engineer under interview conditions, not merely recall AI concepts.

Read an AI/GenAI job description and prioritize preparation accordingly.
Position yourself for AI Engineer, GenAI DS, or related roles.
Whiteboard production RAG, agent, and full AI-system architectures.
Diagnose RAG, agent, fine-tuning, inference, and production failures systematically.
Defend prompt vs RAG vs agent vs fine-tuning decisions.
Reason about cost, latency, scale, privacy, reliability, and operational complexity.
Answer questions about new models/tools without relying on hype.
Present and defend portfolio projects under deep technical questioning.
Communicate assumptions and trade-offs clearly while whiteboarding.
Perform a complete GenAI system-design interview from an ambiguous brief to a defensible architecture.
